Contrasting

While the desire stays the same, the way wealth is turned into power in Musa’s time is polar to the 21st century. The hajj was centered on one ruler and a dramatic event. It was a religious act and a display of power using gold and masses to create a spectacle. Its goal developed over the journey, and resulted in Mali being put on the power map within the Islamic world. Musa's image, generosity, and devotion to Islam were enough to create influence. His actions did not require careful planning and the benefits stemmed from the gold itself. In fact, his journey was so spontaneous that Eygpt was unable to manage the influx of gold in their society, resulting in damage to the local economy. Plus, his decisions to meet with the Sultan were rash and even against his will, although they provided diplomatic benefit. In the modern world, Saudi Arabia has to plan and execute far more carefully through Vision 2030. It relies on huge institutional efforts like the PIF who manages the distribution of trillion dollar backing from oil and deciding to fund long-term investments in various sectors. Saudi Arabia is slowly expanding its influence through coordination instead of splashing it all on a dramatic display.10

Musa had little reputational value to lose, since Mali was not yet widely known, and could spread influence by simply throwing his infinite wealth around. He also navigated friendly lands due to their religious connection through Islam. In a modern world with media, human rights issues, oil price instability, conflict in the Middle East, and huge competition, Saudi Arabia faces an uphill battle. In order to keep its image stable and power growing, its actions must seem controlled and reasonable, and must rely on stability in the economy. Both motives remain similar, but the contrast shows how the same impulse changes form due to the political and power structures of the time period.11
