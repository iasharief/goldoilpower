Contrasting

While the desire stays the same, the way wealth is turned into power is polar in contrast in Musa’s time versus the 21st century. The hajj was centered on one ruler and a dramatic event. It was a religious act and a display of power using gold and masses to create a spectacle and create waves for Mali to be put on the power map within the Islamic world. Musa's image, generosity, and devotion to Islam were enough to create influence. In the modern world, Saudi Arabia has to work much harder through Vision 2030. It relies on huge planning and institutional efforts like PIF and long-term investment in various sectors. Saudi Arabia is slowly expanding its influence through coordination instead of splashing it all on a one-and-done display.

Musa had little to lose, not being on the radar for many, and could spread influence by just throwing the infinite wealth he had around, and he navigated through the already friendly Islamic world. However, Saudi Arabia has had to fight an uphill battle with media, human rights, oil price instability, conflict, and huge competition. It has to defend its image and make itself seem stable. In the end, both show the same desire to transform wealth into lasting influence.
